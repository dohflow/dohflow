# DohFlow trademark policy

DohFlow's **code** is free software under AGPL-3.0. Its **name and logo** are not.

That is deliberate and it is the norm for open-source projects — Rust, Mozilla,
Debian and Python all do the same thing. The reason is narrow: anyone should be
able to use, modify, fork and sell this software, and *nobody* should be able to
put the DohFlow name on a modified build and have users believe it came from us.
The license protects your freedom to change the code. This policy protects users
from being misled about what they are running — which matters more than usual for
software that holds financial data.

**This policy is about identity, never about restricting the code.** If it ever
appears to limit what you may do with the software itself, the license wins.

## What is covered

The name **DohFlow**, the DohFlow logo mark, the wordmark, and the lockups in
`docs/product/brand/`. These are owned by the project maintainer and are **not**
licensed under AGPL-3.0.

Everything else in this repository is under the license in [LICENSE](LICENSE).

## You may do these without asking

- **Say true things.** "DohFlow", in reference to this project, in articles,
  reviews, talks, comparisons, tutorials, or criticism. Favorable or not.
- **"Built with DohFlow", "compatible with DohFlow", "works with DohFlow"** for
  a genuinely compatible or derived tool, so long as it is clear your project is
  yours and not published by us.
- **Redistribute the official unmodified builds** under the DohFlow name — a
  package manager, a mirror, an internal deployment.
- **Use the logo to link to us**, at its published proportions and colors.
- **Say your fork is "derived from DohFlow"** or "a fork of DohFlow". Describing
  lineage is accurate and welcome.

Nominative, truthful, non-confusing reference does not need our permission. It
generally does not need anyone's permission, and this policy does not claim
otherwise.

## Please ask first for these

Email **hello@dohflow.app**.

- **The DohFlow name or logo on a modified build.** Fork freely — the license
  guarantees that — but ship it under your own name. "Derived from DohFlow" is
  fine; *being* DohFlow is not.
- **A commercial product or hosted service named after DohFlow**, or whose name
  or branding could be mistaken for it.
- **Domain names, social handles, or app-store listings** containing "DohFlow".
- **Merchandise** carrying the name or logo.

We are not looking for reasons to say no. Ask, and we will usually say yes or
tell you exactly what to change.

## Using the logo

- Do not recolor it. The palette is fixed: emerald `#006341`, terracotta
  `#DB8F6B`, ink `#1c1815`, and white.
- Do not stretch, rotate, add effects to, or redraw it. Scale it proportionally.
- Do not combine it with your own logo into a new mark.
- Do not use it as your app icon, favicon, or avatar for something that is not
  DohFlow.
- Leave clear space around it — at least the height of the mark's own counter.

Files, and the only ones that are current, are in `docs/product/brand/`.

## Forks

To be unambiguous, because this is the case that actually comes up.

**Fork it, modify it, sell it, host it.** AGPL-3.0 guarantees all of that, and
this policy does not touch any of it. "Free software" includes the freedom to
charge money.

**But the license attaches conditions, and they are the point.** AGPL-3.0 is
strong copyleft: distribute a modified DohFlow and you must offer your users the
complete corresponding source under the same license. **Section 13 extends that
to network use** — run a modified DohFlow as a hosted service and your users are
entitled to your modified source, even though you never "distributed" a binary
to them. A closed-source commercial fork or a proprietary hosted version of
DohFlow is not something this policy forbids; it is something the *license*
forbids. Read [LICENSE](LICENSE) — it governs the code, and it is the document
that matters here.

**Then rename it.** Replace the name and logo with your own, and say "derived
from DohFlow" wherever you like. That single change is this policy's whole ask.

So the honest summary is: the license decides what you may do with the code, and
it is permissive about commerce and strict about source. This file only decides
what you may call the result.

## If we think there is a problem

We will email you and explain what we think is confusing and how to fix it.
Almost everything is solved by a rename or a clarifying line. We are not
interested in lawyers over an honest mistake, and we would rather have your fork
exist than not.

## Changes

This policy may be updated. Changes apply going forward and will not
retroactively make prior good-faith use a violation.
